Annual January Tradition

Design, Build, and Race Your Custom 5-Ounce Racer!

Every January, the scouts of Pack 505 turn raw blocks of pine, plastic wheels, and metal axles into aerodynamic speed machines! The Pinewood Derby is a timeless Scouting tradition that teaches engineering principles, tool safety, physics, sportsmanship, and pride of craftsmanship.

The Pack supplies each registered scout with an official BSA Pinewood Derby car kit. Scouts work with their parents to cut, sand, weight, and paint their creations before competing on our professional electronic 4-lane track with high-precision digital timing gates.

Inspection Standards

Official Car Rules & Inspection Criteria

All cars must pass pre-race inspection and weigh-in on official calibrated scales before competing in speed heats.

⚖️ Weight Limit

5.00 Ounces Maximum (141.7 grams).

Cars weighing over 5.00 oz on the official scale must be drilled or modified before the final check-in deadline.

📏 Dimensions

  • Overall Width: 2 ¾ inches max
  • Overall Length: 7 inches max
  • Underbody Clearance: 3/8 inch min
  • Wheelbase: Must fit track guide rails

🛞 Wheels & Lubrication

  • Official BSA Wheels & Axles only
  • No bearings, springs, or bushings
  • Dry Graphite Lubricant ONLY (no liquid oils or sprays)
💥

Fan Favorite: The Demolition Derby!

After the championship speed heats, scouts can enter their cars into the high-energy, crowd-cheering Demolition Derby heats where cars race until the final pieces cross the finish line!

Engineering Secrets

How to Build a Fast Pinewood Derby Car

Physics is the secret to Pinewood Derby speed! Here are pro tips from veteran Pack 505 builders:

  • Max Out the Weight: Build right up to the 5.00 oz limit using tungsten or lead weights.
  • Center of Gravity: Place the majority of weight 0.75" to 1" in front of the rear axle to maximize potential energy on the slope.
  • Polish the Axles: Sand away the factory ridges and burrs on nail axles using 400 to 2000-grit wet sandpaper and metal polish.
  • Dry Graphite: Work dry powdered graphite thoroughly into each wheel bore and spin them repeatedly.
  • Aerodynamic Shape: A low-profile wedge cuts through air resistance much faster than a tall box.
