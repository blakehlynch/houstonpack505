What is Akela?

Wisdom, Authority, and Dedicated Service

In Cub Scouts, Akela (pronounced Ah-KAY-la) means "Good Leader." Akela is anyone who acts as a mentor and guide to the Scout. At home, parents are Akela. In den meetings, the Den Leader is Akela. During pack meetings, it is the Cubmaster.

This portal provides our trusted adults and registered Scouters with the training, tools, and best practices to deliver a world-class program for our children.

🛡️ Mandatory Training Requirements

All registered adult leaders must maintain current Youth Protection Training (YPT), renewable every two years. All parents are encouraged to take this free 75-minute online course!

Best Practices

Den Leader Playbook & Meeting Strategies

  • Share a Semester Calendar: Set your meeting dates and adventures at the start of Fall and Spring so families can plan ahead.
  • Scoutbook Quick Entry: Use Scoutbook Plus to take roll and record adventure completions immediately after each meeting.
  • Engage Parent Helpers: Invite each parent in your den to lead or co-lead one adventure meeting during the year based on their hobbies or profession!
  • Stagger with Sister Dens: If there is another den of the same rank, coordinate to cover complementary adventures for easy make-up sessions.
  • Den Chiefs: Request an older Scouts BSA youth (Troop 505 / 506) to serve as your Den Chief to run games and model leadership!
  • Keep it Moving: Plan meetings to last 50–60 minutes with hands-on activities, movement, and a brief gathering game.
